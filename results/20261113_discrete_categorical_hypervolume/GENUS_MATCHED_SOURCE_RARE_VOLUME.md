## Distinct lineage/source control estimand

The main 928-island analysis fixes 30 Direct original recorded flower-trait-complete species and compares with a 30-species source native GIFT flora in the same broad region. Its primary **rare minus common** categorical one-trait neighborhood reference coverage difference in the tropics is source-disjoint and robust to observed distinct profile k in two reference nulls. However, this cannot distinguish a change in floral phenotype within source genera from a change in *which genera are recorded*.

Here plant **genus identity** is parsed from the curated original accepted botanical species binomial. For each original island:
1. Identify candidate native plant names from five geographically nearest original GIFT regions that are **different accepted species** from *all* focal original island species, including unresolved original provenance.
2. Before inspecting any morphology frequencies or network outcomes, count the number of independent genus-specific matched plant slots \(\sum_g\min(n_{island,g},n_{source,g})\).
3. The tropical original all-recorded 106-island cohort has only **2 islands** with ≥30 such genus-matched slots; none of 17 tropical strict-native-qualified islands. Hence an exact n30 genus-matched comparison does **NOT** identify a globally applicable tropical effect. Do not lower thresholds to rescue original 30-species assertion.
4. Run a **separately labeled n=15-genus-matched study**: on each eligible island draw exactly 15 Direct species and 15 different candidate-source Direct species, matching the entire GENUS frequency table exactly, with 40 repeated independent thinnings. A restricted matched-source cohort is selected by name-level genus opportunity only, not by flower color, form, symmetry or rarity outcome. Within the same eligible island cohort and the same n15, draw an ordinary source-distant species reference without genus matching, to distinguish original cohort selection from genus-matching shifts.
5. Evaluate rare-minus-common reference-neighborhood coverage and a reference null conditioned on observed profile count k. Reference labels remain **29 rare profile types globally carried by 1–2 species**, **25 common globally carried by ≥26**, 46 middle, from 2,234 Direct original species. A second null uses k original profiles drawn with probabilities proportional to original worldwide Direct species frequencies. Whole-block (10°) and first-nearest-GIFT-source-signature conditional bootstraps each have 1,999 resamples.
6. Do not extrapolate the new n15 restricted comparison to the 106 tropical original islands, to 928 globally, or to true independent taxonomic diversification. Same-genus name matching is not a phylogenetic tree, historical dispersal proof or within-species trait polymorphism test.

**Predeclared minimum to report a regional matched-genus result:** at least 10 individually matched islands, five physical spatial blocks and five independent first-nearest source signatures; otherwise only sample opportunity is reported.

Metric is the source-relative **rare-minus-common categorical one-mismatch support coverage gap**, subtracting the original 399-draw **exact profile-k-specific** uniform original reference expectation. Negative means lower rarity-conditioned support on islands than native GIFT candidate mainland source species. **Numbers are proportions of an original category-bin reference**, not floral abundance, 17D Gaussian KDE volume or pollination-service loss.

95% intervals = conditional equal-physical-block bootstraps from 1,999 draws. Original same-genus tropical source-signature-equal estimate **+0.00462 [−0.04138,+0.05889]**. Northmid source-equal −0.00201 [−0.01524,+0.01208]. **None of the three genus-matched cohorts has a 95% interval excluding zero, whether weighted by physical block or first-GIFT source signature.**

On the **same 14 tropical islands**, imposing exact genus matching changes the k-controlled source-relative gap by **+0.02416 [−0.01619,+0.07474]** in whole-block resampling; this **difference-of-effects interval ALSO INCLUDES ZERO**. Therefore the apparent attenuation after genus restriction is compatible with a genus-composition/source-flora explanation, but **DOES NOT statistically prove genus assembly caused the original gap**. Attrition (106 to 14) and selective matching of distinct source plant taxa are major limitations.

On the **655 northern midlatitude** matchable islands, the ordinary n15 comparison is already non-directional, compared with a previous n30 source-disjoint all-recorded result on 736 islands showing a negative k-relative gap. The change in cohort and target sample size itself changes the estimand. The n15 genus-restricted result therefore must **NOT** be described as causally making a formerly independently significant general effect disappear.

## Implication for island floral functional macroecology

**What survives:** real source-backed globally rare phenotype-profile neighborhood undercoverage in 106 original tropical and 736 northern-midlatitude all-recorded flora under a categorical Hamming metric, equal n=30 plant names, fixed reference profile count k, disjoint species/source pools, conditional geographic/source significance; the all-recorded population exhibits a demonstrable pattern **of recorded floristic categories**.

**What remains unproven:** an island-wide rare phenotype undercoverage effect that is independent of original plant genus/lineage composition, plant nativeness ascertainment, taxonomy source opportunity, true independent archipelago replication, or actual pollinator-guild decline. A fully taxonomically comparable tropical 30-species study exists on only **2 islands** in the original source.

**Provisional macro-ecological framing:** recording and **lineage-compositional filtering** of globally rare floral category-space neighborhoods is a candidate explanation for source-relative repertoire shape, rather than a confirmed within-genus/adaptive floral space contraction.
